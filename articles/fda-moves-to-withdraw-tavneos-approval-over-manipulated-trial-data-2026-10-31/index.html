FDA Moves to Withdraw Tavneos Approval Over Manipulated Trial Data

The agency says ChemoCentryx re-adjudicated pivotal trial results after unblinding. Amgen, which bought the company for nearly $4 billion, now argues the drug should stay on the market.

THE CONTEXT MATTERS

Main report

The US Food and Drug Administration has proposed withdrawing its approval of Tavneos, a treatment for a rare autoimmune form of vasculitis, after concluding that the drug's pivotal trial data were manipulated, Ars Technica reports. The agency set out its position in an April letter to Amgen, which acquired Tavneos developer ChemoCentryx in 2022.[S20]

The dispute traces back to May 2021, when the FDA released a lengthy briefing document on the experimental medication ahead of an advisory committee meeting. The agency was undecided on approval and wanted outside input, including on whether the drug was meaningfully better than existing therapies. Trial participants appeared to have slightly higher rates of symptom remission after one year, but the FDA said the results were hard to interpret because the study design was less than optimal. One reviewer said the drug's effect was "just not well verified by this study."[S20]

The advisory committee voted narrowly to recommend approval, and the FDA greenlit Tavneos on October 7, 2021. A year later, Amgen bought ChemoCentryx for nearly $4 billion. Tavneos sales reached nearly half a billion dollars in fiscal year 2025.[S20]

According to the FDA, the problem lies in how ChemoCentryx handled the trial data. The company's own original analysis found that patients receiving Tavneos had not achieved better one-year remission rates than those on the usual treatment. Employees then unblinded the data, seeing which participants had received the drug and which had received a placebo, and selected a handful of patients whose remission status would be reassessed. The FDA says this re-adjudication violated the study protocol.[S20]

The evidence surfaced in part through a long-running investor lawsuit, which the FDA now credits with uncovering what it considers proof of data manipulation in the pivotal trial. A court hearing was expected on October 29 to consider a proposed $69 million settlement in that case.[S20]

Amgen has pushed back. In a 104-page document submitted to the FDA this summer, the company said ChemoCentryx's re-adjudication does not "align with the approach Amgen would have taken" and called it "unfortunate" that ChemoCentryx did not disclose its actions to the FDA, noting that Amgen itself had been in the dark.[S20]

Tavneos was intended to replace or reduce the use of steroids, which AAV patients may need for months to years. Since the drug reached the market, it has been linked to eight deaths and dozens of hospitalizations, according to Ars Technica. The United Kingdom has announced it will also remove the product from the market.[S20]

The case also highlights how incentives differ between small biotechs and large pharmaceutical companies. Sean Nicholson, an economist at Cornell University, told Undark that a significant number of FDA-approved drugs start at small companies with few or no marketed products, and that large firms increasingly buy or partner with nimble startups rather than develop breakthrough science themselves. David Ridley, a health economist at Duke University's Fuqua School of Business, said small companies face a different set of incentives: a large company with many pipeline products may more readily abandon an experimental drug than fund costly late-stage testing. One advisory committee participant told Undark the 2021 vote was "based upon an assumption that the sponsor was following the rules of the road."[S20]

The FDA's proposal to withdraw approval is not the final step. Amgen's response is now before the agency, and the outcome will determine whether Tavneos remains available in the US.[S20]

Why it matters

The case tests whether the FDA can unwind an approval years after the fact when the underlying trial data are disputed. For Amgen, Tavneos is a product with nearly half a billion dollars in annual sales and a multibillion-dollar acquisition behind it. For AAV patients, withdrawal would remove a steroid-sparing option, though the drug has also been linked to eight deaths and dozens of hospitalizations. For smaller biotechs, the episode sharpens scrutiny of how trial data are handled before a large company acquires the asset.

What happens next

The FDA will weigh Amgen's 104-page response before deciding whether to finalize withdrawal. Amgen has distanced itself from ChemoCentryx's conduct, which could shape whether the agency treats the issue as a company-specific violation or a broader data-integrity problem. The proposed $69 million investor-lawsuit settlement, subject to the October 29 hearing, may also produce further disclosures. The UK's removal decision adds pressure but does not bind the FDA.

What to watch

  • A final FDA decision or order on the Tavneos approval
  • Amgen disclosure in SEC filings or earnings calls about Tavneos revenue and contingency planning
  • Court action on the proposed $69 million investor-lawsuit settlement
  • Any FDA action on other ChemoCentryx-era data or Amgen pipeline reviews
  • UK withdrawal timeline and whether other regulators follow
