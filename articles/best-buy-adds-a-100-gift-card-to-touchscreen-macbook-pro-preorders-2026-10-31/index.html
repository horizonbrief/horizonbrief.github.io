Best Buy adds a $100 gift card to touchscreen MacBook Pro preorders

The retailer is pairing a gift card with preorders of Apple's touch-enabled MacBook Pro, while macOS adds small interface changes for finger input.

THE CONTEXT MATTERS

Main report

Best Buy is bundling a $100 gift card with preorders of the new touchscreen MacBook Pro, according to The Verge, which reported the promotion on October 31, 2026. The offer attaches a store credit to a hardware purchase rather than cutting the sticker price, a structure that keeps the listed price of the laptop intact while giving buyers $100 to spend at Best Buy later.[S1]

The promotion lands as Apple's laptop line moves to touch input, a change that also reaches into software. The Verge reported that macOS includes adjustments meant to make the new touch experience smoother, including magnifying the traffic-light window buttons when a finger moves near them. That detail is small, but it points to the kind of work involved when a desktop operating system built around a pointer and keyboard is used with a finger: targets that are easy to hit with a cursor can be awkward to tap, and interface elements designed for precision need to grow or move when a hand approaches.[S1]

The Verge's report identifies the promotion and the macOS behavior but does not lay out the full terms in the supplied material. It does not specify which MacBook Pro configurations qualify, whether the gift card applies to every preorder or only certain models, when the offer expires, or whether the card can be combined with other discounts. Those omissions matter for anyone comparing the deal against Apple's own store or other retailers, because a gift card is not the same as a cash discount: its value depends on the buyer shopping at Best Buy again, and its usefulness depends on exclusions that are not described here.[S1]

Retailers commonly use gift-card bundles around major hardware launches for several reasons. A gift card preserves the manufacturer's advertised price, which matters when Apple and its authorized resellers are expected to hold a consistent price across channels. It also pulls the buyer back into the retailer's ecosystem, where the card can be spent on accessories, software, services or a later purchase. For a laptop launch, accessories are an obvious destination: docks, cables, cases, external storage and protection plans are common add-ons, and a $100 credit can offset part of that basket. The Verge's report does not say whether Best Buy is steering buyers toward specific accessories, and no such claim should be inferred from the bundle alone.[S1]

The touchscreen MacBook Pro is the central product here, and the macOS detail is the most concrete software change described. Magnifying the traffic-light buttons when a finger approaches is a targeted accommodation rather than a redesign of the whole interface. It suggests Apple is treating touch as an additional input method on the laptop rather than replacing the trackpad and keyboard. That approach would be consistent with a device that still runs desktop software and still expects precise pointing for many tasks, though the supplied reporting does not describe Apple's broader design strategy or confirm how extensively touch is supported across apps.[S1]

For buyers, the practical question is whether the bundle changes the effective cost of the machine. A $100 gift card lowers the net cost only if the buyer would have spent at least $100 at Best Buy anyway. A buyer who plans to purchase the laptop and then buy a dock or external drive from the same retailer gets a straightforward benefit. A buyer who would otherwise buy nothing else, or who prefers another retailer, gets a card with less value than its face amount. The Verge report does not include a price for the touchscreen MacBook Pro, so the relative size of the incentive cannot be calculated from the supplied material.[S1]

For Best Buy, the promotion is a way to compete for preorders at a moment when the product is likely to be available through multiple channels. The retailer is not described as offering a lower price; it is offering a conditional credit. That can protect margin on the hardware while creating a second transaction. Whether the strategy works depends on how many buyers redeem the card and what they buy, figures that are not available at the time of the report.[S1]

The timing is also notable. The report is dated October 31, 2026, and describes the offer as tied to preorders, which means the promotion is running before the product reaches general retail shelves. Preorder promotions often carry deadlines or limited allocations, but the supplied material does not state either. Buyers considering the offer would need to check Best Buy's own terms for the qualifying models, the expiration date and any restrictions on the gift card.[S1]

The macOS change described by The Verge is a reminder that touchscreen laptops depend on software as much as hardware. A touchscreen can be added to a laptop, but the experience improves when buttons, menus and other controls are sized for fingers. Magnifying the window controls is one example. The report does not say whether other elements of macOS received similar treatment, so the scope of the touch adaptation remains unclear from the supplied material.[S1]

Apple has not commented in the supplied reporting on the promotion or on the design rationale for the macOS change. Best Buy has also not provided terms beyond what The Verge described. The report does not include sales figures, preorder volumes or analyst estimates, and none should be assumed.[S1]

What is established is narrower: Best Buy is offering a $100 gift card with preorders of the touchscreen MacBook Pro, and macOS includes at least one interface adjustment for touch input, magnifying the traffic-light buttons when a finger approaches. The rest, including how the bundle compares with other retailers' offers and how buyers will respond, is not yet documented in the supplied material.[S1]
