Recursive Releases Flint, a Free Laptop Coding Model It Says AI Largely Built

The startup says its Eureka Machine assisted nearly every stage of Flint's development, yielding a 40 billion parameter model that runs offline and is free to download under an open source license.

Horizon Brief summary

Recursive, an AI startup founded last year, released its first model on Wednesday. The company, which says it is working toward AI that can improve itself, calls the model Flint: a free coding model that runs on a laptop and was built largely with AI assistance, according to TechCrunch.[S504]

Recursive credits its automated research system, the Eureka Machine, with helping at nearly every stage of Flint's development. The company says that assistance let a small team build the model from scratch in under four months. The Eureka Machine generates ideas, runs experiments, and uses the outcomes to choose what to attempt next.[S504]

As TechCrunch describes it, what Recursive has deployed so far is narrower than its long-term ambition: an AI research assistant supervised by people, which produced a model small enough to run on a laptop. Recursive did not say how much researcher time the Eureka Machine saved, and it is not the only organization turning to AI to support research work.[S504]

Flint is a mixture-of-experts model, meaning it engages only a fraction of its capacity for any given task. It has 40 billion parameters in total but uses 2.5 billion of them at a time. The model is free to download on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 license, which permits free use and modification.[S504]

Because Flint runs on the user's own computer instead of a company's cloud servers, developers can use it offline, keep their code private, and avoid the usage fees that cloud AI services charge based on how much text a model processes, according to TechCrunch.[S504]

In Recursive's own testing, Flint generated about 131 tokens, or chunks of text, per second on a MacBook Pro with Apple's M4 Pro chip. The company says that is roughly twice the 58 to 69 tokens per second that comparable models reached on the same machine, including Google's Gemma 4 and Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 Lightning. Recursive also says Flint outperformed other models under 50 billion parameters on SWE-Bench Verified, a benchmark that measures how well AI can fix real-world software bugs. Both results come from the company rather than independent evaluation.[S504]

Recursive acknowledged that Flint requires more guidance than the large paid coding assistants and suggested that users break large tasks into smaller steps. The release is the first model from a company whose stated goal is self-improving AI, and the Eureka Machine's role in building Flint offers an early, limited example of that approach in practice.[S504]
