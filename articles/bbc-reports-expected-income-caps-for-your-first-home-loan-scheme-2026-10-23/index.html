BBC Reports Expected Income Caps for Your First Home Loan Scheme

The BBC reports that income limits for the government-backed Your First Home equity loan scheme are expected to be confirmed at next week's Budget, with the Conservatives criticizing the plans and developers urging speed.

Horizon Brief summary

Households will need to earn less than £110,000 to qualify for the government's new loan scheme for first-time buyers, or under £140,000 for homes bought in London, according to BBC Business. The earnings limits are expected to be confirmed at the Budget next week, the BBC reported, saying The Times first reported them.[S502]

The programme, called Your First Home, will offer government-backed equity loans covering 20% of the price of new-build properties in England bought from developers taking part in the scheme. The loans will also include an initial interest-free period, and are intended to let buyers purchase a home with deposits as low as 2.5%.[S502]

According to the BBC, earlier schemes aimed at first-time buyers did not restrict eligibility by income. Those programmes drew criticism for contributing to higher house prices by allowing relatively wealthier households to borrow more.[S502]

Prime Minister Andy Burnham introduced the Your First Home scheme at last month's Labour conference, saying it would focus on people who "really need support," the BBC reported.[S502]

Reaction has split along familiar lines. The Home Builders Federation, which represents developers, welcomed the plans and called on the government to set the scheme up quickly. The Conservatives described the proposals as a "colossal admission of failure" on Labour's housebuilding record and raised concerns that a new levy on developers could raise the price of new homes.[S502]
