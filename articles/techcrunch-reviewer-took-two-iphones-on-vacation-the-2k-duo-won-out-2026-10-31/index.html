TechCrunch reviewer took two iPhones on vacation; the $2K Duo won out

Sarah Perez carried the foldable iPhone Duo alongside her iPhone 17 Pro Max as a safety net. She used the Duo more, and came home not wanting to switch back.

THE CONTEXT MATTERS

Main report

TechCrunch's Sarah Perez spent a five-day vacation carrying two phones: the new foldable iPhone Duo and her iPhone 17 Pro Max. She had packed the Pro Max as a safety net after the Duo arrived just as she was leaving. By the end of the trip, she wrote, she was using the Duo more and did not want to go back.[S1]

The Duo costs about $2,000, and Perez wrote that in the days before its ship date she had started doing the math on upgrade options that would let her afford one. She said she hesitated over both the price and the scale of the change from a high-end slab phone to what she described as a more experimental device. She framed the move as carrying a psychological switching cost beyond the sticker price.[S1]

Her frustrations were concrete. Some apps were not yet optimized for the Duo, though she noted companies have been pushing out Duo-optimized updates fairly quickly and that "more than a few" apps still had not. Google Maps was not Duo-optimized during the trip, yet she said she had no trouble using it for driving directions, local search and discovery, and route planning. She expects the major apps to update eventually.[S1]

The bigger complaint was authentication. The Duo lacks Face ID, and Perez called that its biggest drawback, ahead of the screen, the camera and the absence of an Action button for launching an AI assistant. Returning to Touch ID at payment time was cumbersome when her hands were full with shopping bags, a purse or a dog leash. She found the easiest grip was to cradle the phone and authenticate with a thumb.[S1]

On cameras, she was direct about the gap. The Duo has two rear cameras to the Pro Max's three, no telephoto lens, and 10x digital zoom against the Pro's 40x. She still described foldables as whimsical in a way slab phones are not, and said Apple appears to have taken its time on the details, from a barely visible crease to a consistent aspect ratio between screens so content scales proportionally. The Duo also sits inside Apple's software ecosystem, which she said matters for people who move between Apple devices and services and pay for iOS-specific apps.[S1]

Durability came up in the context of the beach. Perez wrote that the Galaxy Z Fold 8 was rated IP48 by comparison, and that she does not expect the Duo to have the same issues, though she hoped a reviewer would take it to the beach soon. She also said she had roughly half a dozen conversations with strangers on vacation, from fellow shoppers to servers to business owners, and expects that attention to diminish as the novelty wears off. She is certain at least one of those strangers had never seen a folding phone before, and argued that Apple shipping one means the concept will no longer be niche but known to the mainstream.[S1]

The account is a single reviewer's experience over five days, not a lab test or a durability trial. It captures the trade-offs a buyer weighing a $2,000 foldable against a mature flagship actually faces: app readiness, biometric convenience, camera range and the social friction of a new form factor.[S1]

Why it matters

A $2,000 foldable only sells if people who already own a top-tier iPhone decide the form factor is worth losing Face ID, a telephoto lens and full app optimization. Perez's account suggests the switching cost is real but surmountable for at least some buyers, which matters to Apple's pricing and to developers deciding how fast to ship Duo-specific updates. It also matters to rivals: if Apple normalizes folding phones, Samsung and others face a mainstream competitor in a category they spent years building alone.

What happens next

Watch whether the app gap Perez described closes. She expects major apps to update, and the pace of those releases will shape how quickly the Duo feels like a normal phone rather than an experiment. Durability reporting, particularly sand and water exposure at beaches, is the other open question, since she flagged the IP rating comparison with the Galaxy Z Fold 8 without testing it herself.

What to watch

  • Duo-optimized updates from major apps, especially mapping and payments
  • Independent durability and water-resistance testing, including beach and sand exposure
  • Apple's next quarterly commentary on iPhone mix and average selling price
  • Whether other reviewers report the same Touch ID friction at checkout
  • Samsung's response in pricing or features for the next Galaxy Z Fold
