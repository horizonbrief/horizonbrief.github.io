Tesla Says It Will Sell Cybercab to Customers by End of Year, TechCrunch Reports

The announcement comes as federal regulators examine whether the vehicle meets safety standards, according to TechCrunch. Tesla's CEO framed the Cybercab as transport-as-a-service rather than a car for owners.

Horizon Brief summary

Tesla said Wednesday that it will begin selling its Cybercab to customers before the end of the year, TechCrunch reported, even as federal regulators are investigating whether the vehicle complies with vehicle safety standards.[S3]

According to TechCrunch, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, the federal auto safety regulator, announced last month that it had opened an investigation into the Cybercab. TechCrunch placed that announcement just hours after the vehicles began operating on public streets in Austin.[S3]

TechCrunch reported that current regulations do not accommodate vehicles without manual controls. The Department of Transportation has recently proposed removing those requirements for vehicles designed to be driven autonomously, according to the report.[S3]

On a Wednesday call, Tesla CEO Elon Musk described the Cybercab as primarily a transport-as-a-service offering, distinct from models such as the 3, the Y and the Roadster that he cast as cars customers would want to own, TechCrunch reported.[S3]

TechCrunch reported that Musk has discussed building a large fleet of low-cost robotaxis for years, but that until recently those plans focused on converting privately owned Tesla vehicles into self-driving taxis through a software update.[S3]

The timing of the sales commitment places it alongside the unresolved regulatory question.[S3]
