Nirva Raises $25M as It Prepares a Voice-Filtering AI Pendant

The startup, founded by two former Meta Reality Labs employees and a fashion entrepreneur, announced a $25 million pre-Series A round. Its $249 pendant is set to take orders in November.

Horizon Brief summary

Nirva, a startup building a jewelry-like wearable pendant, announced a $25 million pre-Series A round, TechCrunch reported. The new funding adds to $8 million the company raised earlier in the year from investors including Lightspeed and South Park Commons, bringing its total raise to $33 million.[S302]

The company was founded in 2025 by Wei Lyu and Jason Chen, both former Meta Reality Labs employees, along with Hanying Hu, who founded a fashion and jewelry brand. Lyu is Nirva's CEO.[S302]

Nirva's first device is a pendant that can also be worn as a bracelet. It weighs about eight grams, which the company presents as making it easy to wear throughout the day, and its battery is rated to last up to three days on a single charge. The device is priced at $249 and will be offered in silver, brushed silver, black and gold.[S302]

According to TechCrunch's reporting, the company's approach to the privacy question that hangs over ambient devices rests on how the product is set up and what it does with captured audio. During onboarding, users are asked to record snippets of their voice, and Nirva builds its model from those samples. In use, the device filters out conversations or data captured from other people around it, according to the company.[S302]

Nirva also says that by default it does not keep audio data or transcripts once conversations have been processed. Before discarding the audio, the company says, it tracks tone, pitch, volume and speed, attributes it describes as useful for deriving emotion-related understandings that are then surfaced in the app.[S302]

The companion app parses the wearer's speech and produces insights the company infers are related to the user's personality. TechCrunch reported that these include patterns, the people who tend to be around the user, the places they have been and other daily notes.[S302]

Privacy is a central concern for ambient devices, since people may not want to be recorded all the time. Nirva's stated design choices, from voice enrollment to filtering and default deletion, are the company's account of how the product addresses that concern.[S302]

Nirva positions itself as a personal insights company rather than a productivity startup. In a call with TechCrunch, Lyu said the company wanted to build a wearable that blends in with fashion while also providing value to users.[S302]

Nirva says orders are expected to open in November. Its stated goal is to dispatch the first units to early users before year-end.[S302]
