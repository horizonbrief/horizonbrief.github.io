UK regulators propose easing City accountability rules

Bank of England and FCA plans would cut reporting on lower-level misconduct and free 140,000 managers from extra checks, with regulators estimating about £2bn in savings over a decade.

Horizon Brief summary

The Bank of England and the Financial Conduct Authority have put forward plans to relax parts of the accountability regime that holds senior City figures accountable for wrongdoing, The Guardian reported. The proposals include reducing reporting requirements for what the report describes as "lower-level misconduct" and would free 140,000 managers from additional checks.[S503]

The regulators said the changes are intended to "significantly" reduce the number of roles subject to enhanced scrutiny. Under the plans, only the most senior executives — among them chief executives and chief financial officers — would be required to demonstrate annually, and before moving into new posts, that they are "fit and proper."[S503]

The proposals were announced as the regulators hosted a dinner for City executives at the Mansion House in central London, according to The Guardian.[S503]

The Bank of England and the FCA estimated that removing what they called "unnecessary burdens" would save businesses about £2bn over ten years. The report said the cost of complying with the Bank of England's and the FCA's reporting requirements under the regime would be cut by £11.5m and £229m a year respectively.[S503]

According to The Guardian, the regulators said the changes would not mean bosses could escape consequences for bad behaviour.[S503]

The move is the latest in a series of City rules to be scaled back since the government gave regulators a new objective of ensuring UK firms are competitive internationally, the report said.[S503]
