Barclays Plans 5,000 Job Cuts as Executives Cite Technology and AI Gains

The bank links the measures to savings planned for 2027. Unite challenges its approach, calling for workers to gain new skills as technology changes.

Horizon Brief summary

Barclays is cutting 5,000 jobs in a wider effort to reduce costs, The Guardian reports. That represents about 5% of its worldwide workforce of 93,000. Executives said 1,000 workers had already been cut since the beginning of the year.[S501]

Chief financial officer Anna Cross linked the measures to a plan for more than £1.1bn in savings in 2027. She put the share of the group's actions concerning support functions at around 60%, with fewer developers among the changes, and credited AI with making staff more productive.[S501]

The bank's explanation includes more than AI alone. Asked about the number of cuts directly attributable to the technology, chief executive CS Venkatakrishnan pointed to investment in technology and simpler processes. He described AI as a component of the bank's modernisation, with a potentially larger role in future. The 2027 date relates to the savings target.[S501]

Unite challenged the bank's approach. General secretary Sharon Graham accused Barclays of using AI to justify discarding experienced employees. She argued for improving workers' skills so they could remain employed as technology changed, and criticized the bank's short-term decisions, according to The Guardian.[S501]

The report also described an earlier announcement by Standard Chartered. That bank planned to eliminate more than 7,000 jobs over the next four years as it increased its use of AI. The planned reduction would affect about 15% of its back-office positions by 2030.[S501]
