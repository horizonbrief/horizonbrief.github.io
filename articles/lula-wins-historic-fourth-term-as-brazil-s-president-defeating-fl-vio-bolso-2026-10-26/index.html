Lula Wins Historic Fourth Term as Brazil's President, Defeating Flávio Bolsonaro

With 100% of votes counted, Lula took 51.79% to Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.21%, a margin of 4.4 million votes. Bolsonaro refused to concede and said his legal team would seek to annul the result.

THE CONTEXT MATTERS

Main report

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won a historic fourth term as Brazil's president, defeating the far-right challenger Flávio Bolsonaro, according to The Guardian. With 100% of votes counted, the 80-year-old leftist took 51.79% of the vote to his 45-year-old rival's 48.21%, a margin of 4.4 million votes. The Guardian reports that Brazil's electoral authority confirmed the result, completing a turnaround from the first round earlier in October.[S1][S2]

The campaign's first round, in early October, went against Lula: he narrowly lost to Flávio Bolsonaro, plunging progressive Brazilians into what The Guardian described as three weeks of despair. The second-round reversal is, by the outlet's account, without precedent since democracy returned to Brazil in the 1980s — never before had a first-round runner-up gone on to win the second vote. Lula's victory speech, delivered to a packed crowd of supporters in a São Paulo conference hall, framed the outcome in broad terms. He said his government would change the way the country is governed, called the election a defeat for denialism, thanked God and the Brazilian people, and said it was democracy that made the result happen.[S1][S2]

Flávio Bolsonaro said during Lula’s victory speech that he could not concede and that his legal team would seek annulment. He objected to Justice Cristiano Zanin’s decision to extend voting by two hours, to 7pm, describing it as a change in the rules during the contest. The Guardian notes that Bolsonaro’s own campaign had also requested an extension. His announced intention to challenge the outcome is separate from any decision by a court to alter the reported result.[S1][S2]

Flávio Bolsonaro is the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro. The Guardian reports that Jair lost the 2022 election to Lula and was subsequently jailed for plotting a coup. The younger Bolsonaro’s challenge concerns the new election, rather than that earlier contest.[S1][S2]

BBC News traces Lula’s rise from a childhood of poverty to metalworking and trade-union leadership. Before becoming a politician, he sold peanuts and shined shoes, learned to read at 10 and later trained for industrial work near São Paulo. A workplace accident in the 1960s cost him the little finger of his left hand. In 1975 he was elected to lead the Metalworkers’ Union, with roughly 100,000 members. The BBC describes his role in making it a more independent force. He helped found the Workers’ Party in 1980 with unionists, intellectuals and Church activists. The party later moved toward a more pragmatic social-democratic programme, according to the profile.[S1][S2]

After losing presidential bids in 1989, 1994 and 1998, Lula won in 2002. His first two terms ran from 2003 to 2010. The BBC associates that period with expanded social programmes, minimum-wage increases above inflation and the Bolsa Família family grant. It reports that the assistance reached about 44 million people and helped underpin his support among poorer Brazilians. The profile also records criticism that those programmes did not resolve deeper causes of poverty, including inadequate education. At the end of the two terms, the BBC puts his popularity at 83% and describes a sustained period of economic growth and greater international prominence for Brazil.[S1][S2]

Lula’s political return also followed imprisonment and overturned convictions. According to the BBC, he maintained his innocence and called the accusations politically motivated. He was released in 2019 after just over 18 months, following a Supreme Court ruling concerning imprisonment before appeals were exhausted. His convictions were annulled in 2021, allowing him to contest the 2022 election against Jair Bolsonaro. When he returned to office in 2023, he said it would be his last term. The Workers’ Party nevertheless endorsed him again in 2026. He turns 81 on Tuesday, the BBC reports.[S1][S2]

Policy questions accompany the win. The BBC reports that Lula's support for exploratory oil drilling in offshore waters near the mouth of the Amazon has drawn criticism. Lula insists he remains committed to eventually phasing out fossil fuels and argues oil revenues are key to financing Brazil's transition to clean energy. His government has pointed to reduced deforestation in the Amazon, with satellite data suggesting the area deforested in the first half of 2026 was the lowest in the past decade, as proof its policies were working. The BBC also describes Brazil’s participation in Brics and Lula’s defence of continued ties with Russia and China. He regards that grouping as a potential counterweight to Western influence, according to the profile.[S1][S2]

International reaction was quick. Mexican president Claudia Sheinbaum said democracy had won and congratulated Lula, while Paraguayan president Santiago Peña saluted the Brazilian people for what he called a great democratic journey, according to The Guardian.[S1][S2]

The next term also brings questions over Amazon policy, oil exploration and international alignment.
