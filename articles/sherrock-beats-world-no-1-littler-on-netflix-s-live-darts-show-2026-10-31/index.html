Sherrock beats world No 1 Littler on Netflix's live darts show

Fallon Sherrock defeated Luke Littler on Beat Luke Littler at Manchester Central, a result the streamer had planned contingency programming for in case the world champion fell early.

THE CONTEXT MATTERS

Main report

Fallon Sherrock beat Luke Littler, the world darts No 1, on Netflix's live programme Beat Luke Littler at a packed Manchester Central arena, the Guardian reported. The show, hosted by Gary Lineker and Richard Osman, was built around the possibility that someone could take on the world champion; Sherrock did exactly that.[S5]

Netflix had no guarantee when or whether Littler would be beaten, according to the Guardian, and had put contingency plans in place for a second-place competition in case he lost within the first few rounds. That planning indicates the producers treated an early exit by the headline player as a live possibility rather than a remote one.[S5]

Sherrock's participation had drawn some controversy when it was revealed earlier in October. She holds an MBE for services to darts, and critics argued she was not a true amateur given her appearances at the PDC World Darts Championship and her win in the inaugural Women's World Matchplay in 2022. The Guardian reported that background without resolving the dispute.[S5]

After the win in Manchester, Sherrock described the period leading up to it as difficult. "I mean, it's been tough," she said. Speaking to Lineker, she added: "I can't put into words how much this means." The Guardian reported that the result was greeted with huge cheers from the audience and a hug from her young son.[S5]

The programme was a risk for the streamer, the Guardian reported, but sources said audience numbers stayed steady despite competition from The Celebrity Traitors on the BBC, and appeared to rise after the BBC show ended. Viewers could vote on the order in which players appeared.[S5]

Workerbee's managing director, Michelle Chappell, told the Guardian that the idea came from CEO Rick Murray: "Wouldn't it be amazing if someone could take on Luke Littler." Chappell described the format as "a sport event but a big show at the same time" with "a sense of humour and real jeopardy."[S5]

One winner was guaranteed in the scheduling battle between Beat Luke Littler and The Celebrity Traitors: Banijay, the global production company that owns the companies making both shows, according to the Guardian.[S5]

The Guardian's report does not give the final score, the stage of the competition at which Sherrock beat Littler, or confirmed audience figures for the broadcast. Those details were not available in the supplied material.[S5]

Why it matters

A live Netflix event built around one star carries concentrated risk: if Littler exits early, the format has to hold without him. The Guardian's reporting that contingency programming existed, and that audience numbers held steady against The Celebrity Traitors, suggests the producers designed for that scenario rather than assuming the champion would stay in. For Sherrock, beating the world No 1 on a mainstream streaming broadcast raises her profile beyond darts audiences, though the earlier debate over her amateur status shows that her record invites scrutiny as well as attention. For Netflix, the result is a test of whether a single-match celebrity format can absorb an upset and still draw viewers.

What happens next

Netflix and Workerbee will likely assess whether the event format warrants further live darts programming, with the audience data cited by the Guardian as the main internal measure. Sherrock's win may generate more broadcast and exhibition invitations, while Littler's schedule and response are not addressed in the supplied reporting. Any confirmed viewing figures or a decision on future events would clarify whether the contingency planning proved unnecessary or central to the show's appeal.
