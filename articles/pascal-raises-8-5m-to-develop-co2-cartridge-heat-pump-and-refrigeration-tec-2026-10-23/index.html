Pascal raises $8.5M to develop CO2-cartridge heat pump and refrigeration tech

The startup says its cartridge design could be about 20% more efficient than existing models, and it has signed a pilot with a multinational beverage company.

Horizon Brief summary

Pascal, a startup developing an alternative approach to heat pumps and refrigeration, has raised $8.5 million in a follow-on to its seed round, the company told TechCrunch exclusively. Sazze Partners and LB Investment co-led the round, with KDB Silicon Valley also participating.[S501]

TechCrunch identifies Slavney as Pascal's co-founder and CEO, and Seo as its co-founder and CTO. TechCrunch reported that the funding came together earlier than expected because Pascal landed a customer contract ahead of schedule.[S501]

That contract is with a multinational beverage company for a pilot of the technology, TechCrunch reported.[S501]

Pascal's design departs from the approach of compressing a solid block of material, which TechCrunch describes as one line of research into solid-state refrigerants. Instead, Pascal's heat pumps compress carbon dioxide gas into a cartridge containing a proprietary sponge-like material. As the gas works into the porous material, molecules inside the pores bounce off the walls, and those collisions transfer energy to the material, warming it. A heat exchanger then exhausts some of that heat.[S501]

Run in reverse, the material absorbs energy from the heat exchanger and transfers it to the carbon dioxide molecules. In a Pascal refrigerator, two cartridges run at any given time: one absorbs heat from inside the refrigerator and transfers it to the carbon dioxide, while the other takes heat from the carbon dioxide and moves it to the outside air. Eventually the cartridges become too hot and too cold, so the flow is reversed.[S501]

Slavney told TechCrunch that a refrigerator or heat pump using Pascal's material promises to be around 20% more efficient than existing models. TechCrunch noted that heat pumps and refrigerators use the same basic technology.[S501]

On durability, Slavney said the material is "very robust" and has been cycled up to 300,000 times, which he described as the equivalent of about a year or so in operation.[S501]

Pascal is on track to deliver its first prototype early next year, according to TechCrunch, and hopes to follow that with a batch of more polished refrigerators in 2028. Those dates and the efficiency figure are the company's stated targets.[S501]
