OpenAI Opens Decisions API to All Customers, Challenging TypeSafe's Jev

The API, announced at OpenAI's September developer day, became available to all customers Thursday. OpenAI says it reconfigured ChatGPT Luna rather than building a dedicated decision model.

Horizon Brief summary

OpenAI has made its Decisions API available to all customers, TechCrunch reported, opening a product aimed at fast, low-cost decision-making to the general market. The API was announced at the company's developer day on September 29 and became available to all customers on Thursday, according to the report.[S503]

The release puts OpenAI in competition with TypeSafe, the startup behind the Jev model. TechCrunch describes Jev as having created a category of deep learning models that function as super classifiers: given a set of options, they can make choices quickly and provide a confidence rating for each decision. TypeSafe, founded by a former researcher at the frontier lab, has been valued at $7.6 billion, the report said.[S503]

Nikunj Handa, the product lead for OpenAI's APIs, told TechCrunch that colleagues who saw Jev concluded there were many potential applications. "We have like a million use cases for this," Handa said, describing the reaction. His team moved quickly to produce its own offering.[S503]

Rather than building a specifically calibrated decision model, the team took ChatGPT Luna and reconfigured it to speed up inference and lower cost, according to the report. Handa characterized the result plainly: "Right now, it's just plain old Luna that gets the job done."[S503]

OpenAI also added voice and visual modalities to the public version and worked to reduce latency in regions around the world by targeting inference resources geographically, TechCrunch reported.[S503]

One customer cited in the report is Speak, which builds AI language tutors. Andrew Hsu, the company's chief technology officer, said the Decisions API cut the time between actions from 1.4 seconds to .13 seconds.[S503]

The report notes that it remains unclear what exactly makes a good decision model beyond whether it works for a given user. Optimization across intelligence, cost, calibration and speed appears to differ by workload, according to TechCrunch.[S503]

Handa suggested the market may expand as decision models surface applications that were previously overlooked. In his view, there will be a growing set of use cases that had been hiding in plain sight until decision models revealed them, the report said.[S503]
