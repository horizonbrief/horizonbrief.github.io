Mach9 raises $34M Series A after pivot to AI-assisted CAD software

The company, founded in 2021 as Mach9 Robotics, says nearly 100 private-beta customers have used its AI CAD tool on more than 2,500 projects. First Round Capital and Bessemer Venture Partners co-led the round.

Horizon Brief summary

Mach9 has raised a $34 million Series A round co-led by First Round Capital and Bessemer Venture Partners, the company announced, according to TechCrunch. Prudence, an early-stage New York firm that invests in AI software use cases for construction and real estate, and Quiet Capital, a venture firm already on Mach9's cap table, also participated.[S502]

The funding follows a change of direction. When Alex Baikovitz co-founded the company as Mach9 Robotics in 2021, the goal was to give civil engineers a better, faster and cheaper way to carry out visual inspections. The company later dropped "Robotics" from its name and pivoted to developing AI-powered computer-aided design software instead.[S502]

According to TechCrunch, that bet has produced nearly 100 customers for the private beta of Mach9's software, and those users have applied it to more than 2,500 projects to date.[S502]

Baikovitz described CAD as one of the slowest and most labor-intensive parts of the civil engineering pipeline. He told TechCrunch that delivering a project can take months, and that manual work in legacy incumbent software built by companies such as Autodesk and Bentley Systems runs at roughly a week per mile. Mach9 says its software automates much of that work, making the process 10 times faster and sharply reducing costs.[S502]

On the technical side, Baikovitz said Mach9 developed and trained its own in-house AI models to reach the centimeter-level accuracy that construction and infrastructure work requires. He described the development effort since 2024 as centered on building models that can turn imagery, or combined image and laser-scan datasets, into survey-grade geometry, tracing features such as curbs and pavement striping to the precision and format customers expect.[S502]

In use, the system takes survey data and produces a first draft of a plan, which engineers then refine. Baikovitz said the software gives users tools to draw, remove and adjust vertices and to assess the quality of the AI-generated model, so that licensed professionals can put their names behind the deliverable. He pointed to the liability involved in the industry and the need for licensed professionals who understand what they are reviewing.[S502]

As for the new funding, Baikovitz said Mach9 will divide it between improving the product, hiring industry experts and scaling "all parts of our organization to keep up with where the market is taking us."[S502]
