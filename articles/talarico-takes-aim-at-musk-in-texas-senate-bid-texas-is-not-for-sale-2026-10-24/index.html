Talarico Takes Aim at Musk in Texas Senate Bid: 'Texas Is Not for Sale'

The Democratic Senate candidate rallied at his alma mater, framing his race against Attorney General Ken Paxton around small-dollar fundraising and a single billionaire backer.

THE CONTEXT MATTERS

Main report

James Talarico, the Democratic candidate for the US Senate in Texas, used a campaign rally at his alma mater to take direct aim at Elon Musk, warning that the tech billionaire is trying to buy political influence in the state and declaring that "Texas is not for sale," according to The Guardian. The event at the University of Texas at Austin on Friday packed an auditorium with hundreds of students and other supporters who cheered, whooped and waved campaign signs, giving the 37-year-old state representative a stage to sharpen the central argument of one of the most closely watched races in next month's midterm elections to decide control of Congress.[S6]

Talarico, a Presbyterian seminarian, is running an insurgent campaign against Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, a staunch ally of Donald Trump. The Guardian reported that Democrats are seeking their first statewide win in Texas since 1994, a drought that gives the Senate contest significance well beyond the candidates themselves. Back on the campaign trail after a period away, Talarico set out on a "Clear Eyes, Full Hearts" tour of the state and held a CNN town hall with sceptical Republicans and independent voters, according to the outlet's account.[S6]

Speaking against the backdrop of a giant Texas state flag and with about 40 young supporters holding "Talarico" signs behind him, the candidate struck what The Guardian described as his usual upbeat message while pulling no punches in denouncing Paxton. Talarico described his opponent, who was impeached by his own party over corruption accusations and went through a very public divorce, as the most corrupt politician in the state's history. "I have a legislative record; Ken Paxton has a criminal record," Talarico said, dressed in a dark blue jacket, white open-collar shirt and blue jeans.[S6]

The sharpest passage of the evening was directed at Musk. According to The Guardian, Talarico said the world's richest man was almost single-handedly bankrolling the effort to install Paxton in the US Senate and to stop the movement the Democrat said he is building in the state. He contrasted his own funding base with his opponent's, saying his movement is funded by 1.3 million individuals while Paxton is funded by "one dude."[S6]

The Guardian traced the financing through federal filings. Its account says Musk donated $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund; Texas PAC, a Republican super PAC backing Paxton, subsequently received an equal amount from that fund. Musk is based in Texas, where several of his businesses, including Tesla, SpaceX and X, have relocated. Talarico has sought to offset the outside-spending disadvantage with grassroots fundraising. For the July–September period, Talarico's campaign reported receipts of $64 million. It said 97% of contributions were smaller than $100 and that its fundraising exceeded Paxton's by more than three to one. Those campaign-reported totals are cited here through The Guardian; Horizon Brief has not independently audited them.[S6]

The rally came amid a run of difficult coverage for Paxton. The Wall Street Journal published a video showing Paxton celebrating New Year's Eve in Dubai with two men described as US fugitives, Anosh Ahmed and Sameer Suhail, who were indicted in Illinois on healthcare-fraud charges, according to The Guardian's account of the reporting. Ahmed donated $190,000 to Paxton, including $25,000 after his indictment, the outlet reported. Paxton, meanwhile, launched an 18-day bus tour of Texas.[S6]

The race appears competitive in the polling available so far. The RealClearPolitics average for polls conducted from 6 to 19 October put Talarico ahead by 1.8 percentage points, according to The Guardian. Separately, an Emerson College Polling/Nexstar Media survey released on Thursday found that 48% of voters support Talarico while 46% favour Paxton. The Guardian also noted that Texas Democrats have had their hopes dashed before, notably when former congressman Beto O'Rourke lost his race to Republican Sen. Ted Cruz.[S6]

Early voting began on 19 October, giving both campaigns an immediate test of their ability to mobilise supporters. Talarico voted earlier on Friday, before taking the stage at the Austin rally. Supporters who attended described a mix of impatience with the state's Republican leadership and hope that the candidate's message could mark a turning point. Alina Anil, a 19-year-old University of Texas student who had already cast her ballot, told The Guardian she attended to see Talarico in person and demonstrate her support, saying the state had been red for a long time and that little had come of it. Another supporter, identified as Walker, asked what there was not to like or love about the candidate. Patrick Pearson, 46, an assistive technology professional, said he wanted to back Talarico because of his commitment to policy change, telling the outlet that from everything he had read and heard, the candidate sounded impressive.[S6]

Why it matters

This rally crystallized the central frame of one of the most closely watched Senate races in the 2026 midterms: a grassroots-funded Democrat, James Talarico, casting Attorney General Ken Paxton as the beneficiary of a single billionaire, Elon Musk, while Democrats chase their first statewide win in Texas since 1994. The contest matters beyond Texas because control of Congress is at stake, and because the outcome will test whether a small-dollar, anti-oligarchy message can overcome a large outside-spending advantage in a red state. The race is close in the polling cited, with early voting already under way, so the final stretch is likely to be dominated by mobilization, advertising and scrutiny of both candidates' finances and associations.

What happens next

Early voting is already open, so both campaigns will focus on turnout operations and paid communication through Election Day. Expect continued emphasis from Talarico on small-dollar fundraising and Musk's spending, and from Paxton on his bus tour and Republican-leaning messaging. Further reporting on the Dubai video and on super PAC transfers is plausible, and additional polls may clarify whether Talarico's narrow lead holds.

What to watch

  • Whether additional public polls confirm or reverse Talarico's roughly 1.8-point average lead and the 48-46 Emerson/Nexstar margin
  • Early-voting turnout figures, especially in Democratic-leaning urban counties around Austin, Houston and Dallas
  • Any further federal filings or reporting detailing the $158 million Musk-linked transfer to Texas PAC and subsequent spending
  • Developments in coverage of Paxton's Dubai trip and the donors described as indicted US fugitives
  • Whether Talarico's campaign-reported $64 million quarter and 97% sub-$100 share translate into late advertising and field capacity
